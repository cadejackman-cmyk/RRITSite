What we handle

Four things that keep the line moving

Machines kept separate from office PCs

On most shop floors, an infected laptop in accounts has a clear path to your production equipment. We put them in separate zones so that can't happen.

Wi-Fi that works in a metal building

Steel, racking and machinery wreck wireless coverage, and a floor plan won't predict it. We walk the plant and place equipment for the real building.

Old machines that can't be updated

Nearly everyone has one running software from a decade ago on a machine that cost six figures. We wall it off safely instead of telling you to replace it.

Work scheduled around your shifts

Updates, reboots and changes happen during planned downtime or changeovers - agreed against your production calendar, not against it.

Your ERP is the one that can't go down

If it's down you can't receive material, release work orders, ship or invoice.

So it's monitored constantly, updated only in windows you've agreed, and backed up with a recovery time that matches what a stopped line costs. If your ERP is hosted by the vendor, the priority shifts to backup internet and reliable logins - where those setups usually fail.

Questions

Common questions

Can you separate the network without stopping production?

Yes. We build the new setup alongside the old one and move equipment across in small groups, using scheduled downtime or changeovers. We never attempt it in one big cutover.

We have machines running very old software. What can we do?

Wall them off. The machine goes on its own isolated zone with no internet access and only the specific connections it genuinely needs, plus a backup of its settings so it can be restored rather than rebuilt from memory.

Can you support our ERP system?

We support everything it runs on - servers, network, backups and the terminals people use it from - and work with your ERP vendor on the software itself.
